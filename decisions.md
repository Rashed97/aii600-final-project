## 2026-10-28: Forecast weekly flu admissions in Virginia to book surge nursing shifts

- Decision. The quantity is confirmed influenza admissions to Virginia hospitals in the coming week, from the CDC NHSN state-week series, with the NSSP emergency-department flu visit share as a leading indicator. The decision is how many surge nursing shifts a hospital system books a week ahead, scored as a newsvendor cost in shifts and dollars.
- Why. The series is public domain, so it can go on the `data` release; it has six full seasons (2020–21 through 2025–26) with peaks from 49 to 1,315 admissions a week, so there is both a strong seasonal signal and a held-out season that never enters fitting; and the decision has an asymmetric loss (understaffing costs more than an idle shift), so the action rule is a quantile, not the mean, which is the course's threshold-from-loss idea. A known provenance issue, the May–November 2024 voluntary-reporting window where as few as 26 hospitals reported, gives a concrete bias-versus-noise discussion.
- Alternative. Forecast morning departures at one Capital Bikeshare station to decide overnight rebalancing. Same decision shape and far more rows, but the data license forbids redistributing the files, the decision maker's costs are harder to pin down, and 300 weekly points we can name beat 600,000 rides we cannot.
- Revisit. If the statewide count turns out to be too coarse to stand in for one hospital system (for example, if the NSSP health-service-area series shows the Northern Virginia peak lands weeks away from the state's), switch the quantity to the Fairfax–Arlington health service area's ED visit share and rebuild the loss around that. If the 2024 reporting gap cannot be rescaled defensibly, fit on 2022–23 and 2023–24 only and say so.

## 2026-11-11: Freeze both CDC feeds as of 11 November 2026 and keep every column

- Decision. The analysis runs on one snapshot of the two CDC feeds, taken 11 November 2026 with `fetch_data.py`, whose last week ends 31 October 2026. The files are on the `data` release, their SHA-256 hashes are in `data/README.md`, and `flu.verify_data()` stops the notebook if a file is missing or differs. The files are the source rows for Virginia with every column (322 and 20); choosing and renaming columns happens in `flu.py`, where it can be read. For the emergency-department series the statewide row (`county = All`) is the default.
- Why. Both sources are revised every Wednesday and the hospital feed is labelled preliminary. In this snapshot the hospitals reporting over the last four weeks are 91, 91, 87, 82, so the newest counts are incomplete and will change. Reading a moving source would change the held-out scores from one run to the next. Keeping the raw rows means no choice about the data is hidden inside the download.
- Alternative. Read the API live each time the notebook runs: always current, but no result could be reproduced and the last weeks would change under us. Or store only the six columns `flu.py` uses: smaller files, but it would hide columns a later entry may want, such as the admissions per 100,000 and the share of hospitals reporting.
- Revisit. Take a second snapshot before the final checkpoint. Comparing the two measures how much the last few weeks get revised, which is an error a real one-week-ahead forecaster faces and this snapshot cannot show. If the revisions are large, the baseline and the model have to be scored on what was known at the time. Switch the emergency-department series to the Fairfax-Arlington health service area if the quantity moves to Northern Virginia (see the 28 October entry).
